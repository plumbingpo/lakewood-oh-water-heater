Many of the installation calls we get in Lakewood, OH are not replacements at all. A family adds a bathroom and needs more capacity. A homeowner wants the heater out of a hall closet and into the garage or basement. A remodel calls for a second unit at the far end of the house, or an all-electric conversion means trading gas for a heat pump model. What these jobs share is new work: gas lines that must be sized for the load, vents that must be routed and pitched correctly, circuits that must match the heater, and water lines that must be run cleanly. Lakewood Water Heater works all of that out before the installation starts.

Sizing and placement shape most of the outcome. For a tank, the first-hour rating is the number we compare against your household's busiest hour; for a tankless unit, we size by flow rate and winter temperature rise. Location comes next. A gas heater needs a vent route and combustion air, a heat pump heater needs open space and a condensate drain, and a whole-house electric tankless unit usually needs a big dedicated circuit and matching panel capacity. Put a heater above a finished ceiling and it needs a drain pan; put a gas unit in a garage and code may require a raised platform. You get the trade-offs in plain terms, with no pressure.

On installation day, nothing is left to guesswork. New gas piping is run and pressure-tested, a sediment trap goes on, and the vent is routed exactly to spec. For electric and heat pump units, the circuit is matched to the load. Water lines get accessible shutoffs and proper metal transitions, and the relief valve discharge runs full size to a safe point. Where code requires an expansion tank, drain pan, or seismic straps, we add them, and we pull the permit when your jurisdiction needs one. Prevention Tips That Actually Help

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Glance at the pan and discharge pipe every month or two; water there means it is time to call
  • Plan an annual descaling if you installed a tankless unit
  • Keep flammable liquids and clutter away from gas heaters
  • Set the thermostat near 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Keep a clear path to the heater so it can be serviced and inspected easily
  • Flush a tank every six to twelve months to keep sediment from settling
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years to protect the tank
