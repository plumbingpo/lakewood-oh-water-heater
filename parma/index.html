Two situations call for quick action from you before our technician gets there. The first is a leak: close the cold water valve above the tank and shut off the power or gas, so the tank stops refilling and stops heating. The second is a relief valve that keeps releasing water or a tank that makes loud banging noises: both can mean pressure or temperature running too high, so turn the heater off and stay clear of the discharge line. If there's a gas smell, get outside and call your gas utility first. After that, call us at (216) 247-2721. Those few minutes can prevent a far bigger repair.

How Parma Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year if their water is hard.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually encodes the month and year it was built, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank models make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units around 20. If your heater is past that point and also shows discolored water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement once the tank fails.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Take a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a surprise cleanup.
